# Skill: Top-Tier Operations Research Paper Development

## Skill Name

Top-Tier Operations Research Paper Development

---

## Objective

Develop a publishable research paper from scratch in Operations Research, Optimization, Metaheuristics, Machine Learning for Optimization, or related fields, targeting top journals and conferences such as TEVC, COR, CIE, SWAM, JOC, UTD24 journals, and leading AI conferences.

The objective is not merely to improve benchmark performance, but to identify a meaningful research gap, design a mechanism-driven innovation, validate its effectiveness through rigorous experimentation, and construct a convincing scientific narrative.

---

## Success Criteria

A research project is considered successful only if:

* The research gap is meaningful and practically relevant.
* The motivation is convincing.
* The innovation is mechanism-driven rather than component-combination-driven.
* Experimental results demonstrate clear advantages.
* The proposed mechanism is supported by evidence.
* Contributions meet the expected level of the target venue.
* The work is reproducible and publication-ready.

---

## Workflow

### Stage 1: Research Opportunity Discovery

#### Goal

Identify valuable and publishable research opportunities.

#### Tasks

* Investigate recent publication trends.
* Investigate emerging topics.
* Analyze editorials, keynote talks, surveys, and research roadmaps.
* Identify limitations of existing methods.
* Identify real-world scenarios where existing methods fail.

#### Key Questions

* What specific scenario causes existing methods to perform poorly?
* Why do they fail?
* Which assumptions break down?
* Does this scenario exist in practice?
* Is the problem important enough to justify research?

#### Outputs

* Research gap candidates.
* Literature review notes.
* Problem positioning statement.

---

### Stage 2: Research Gap Evaluation

#### Goal

Determine whether the identified gap deserves investigation.

#### Evaluation Dimensions

##### Motivation

Can the necessity of the study be clearly explained?

##### Research Taste

Does the problem reflect a meaningful scientific challenge?

##### Feasibility

Can the project be implemented with available resources?

##### Technical Difficulty

What obstacles are expected?

What contingency plans exist?

##### Novelty

Verify novelty through extensive literature review.

Avoid simple component combinations.

Avoid superficial hybridization.

##### Contribution

Determine whether contributions are:

* Significant
* Generalizable
* Scientifically meaningful
* Relevant to the broader community

#### Outputs

* Final research problem.
* Contribution statement.
* Innovation roadmap.

---

### Stage 3: Mechanism Design

#### Goal

Create a novel mechanism that addresses the identified bottleneck.

#### Principles

Avoid:

* Component stacking.
* Arbitrary hybrid methods.
* Incremental modifications.

Prefer:

* Bottleneck discovery.
* First-principles reasoning.
* Mechanism-level innovation.
* Explainable design.

#### Required Analysis

* What is the bottleneck?
* Why does it occur?
* Why should the proposed mechanism work?
* What theoretical intuition supports the mechanism?

#### Outputs

* Mechanism description.
* Framework architecture.
* Theoretical rationale.

---

### Stage 4: Experimental Development

#### Goal

Build rigorous and reproducible experiments.

#### Requirements

##### Engineering Quality

* Modular codebase.
* Robust logging.
* Checkpoint saving.
* Resume capability.
* Experiment tracking.
* Configuration management.

##### Fair Comparison

* Same computational budget.
* Same benchmark instances.
* Same stopping criteria.
* Fair parameter tuning.

##### Reproducibility

* Fixed random seeds.
* Version control.
* Detailed experiment records.

#### Outputs

* Experimental platform.
* Benchmark suite.
* Reproducible codebase.

---

### Stage 5: Performance Validation

#### Goal

Verify effectiveness through quantitative evidence.

#### Required Experiments

* Main comparison experiments.
* Statistical significance tests.
* Convergence analysis.
* Sensitivity analysis.
* Scalability analysis.
* Robustness analysis.
* Ablation studies.
* Case studies.

#### Decision Rule

If performance does not meet expectations:

* Diagnose bottlenecks.
* Reevaluate assumptions.
* Refine mechanism.
* Modify framework if necessary.

Do not proceed to paper writing until results are convincing.

#### Outputs

* Performance evidence.
* Statistical evidence.
* Experimental conclusions.

---

### Stage 6: Mechanism Validation

#### Goal

Demonstrate why the method works.

#### Procedure

Observation

↓

Hypothesis

↓

Intervention

↓

Evidence Collection

↓

Mechanism Validation

#### Requirements

Performance improvement alone is insufficient.

The paper must explain:

* Why improvement occurs.
* Which mechanism causes the improvement.
* Under what conditions the mechanism is effective.
* Under what conditions it may fail.

#### Outputs

* Mechanism evidence chain.
* Interpretability analysis.
* Explanatory experiments.

---

### Stage 7: Scientific Writing

#### Preconditions

Only begin writing when:

* Research gap is validated.
* Innovation is finalized.
* Experiments are complete.
* Mechanism is explained.

#### Tasks

Study target venue:

* Writing style.
* Paper structure.
* Figure style.
* Typical contribution patterns.
* Typical article length.

#### Introduction Requirements

Answer:

* Why is the problem important?
* Why is it difficult?
* Why do existing methods fail?
* Why should the proposed mechanism work?

#### Contribution Requirements

Clearly articulate:

* Problem contribution.
* Method contribution.
* Mechanism contribution.
* Experimental contribution.

#### Figure Requirements

Figures should support:

* Problem understanding.
* Framework understanding.
* Experimental interpretation.
* Mechanism explanation.

#### Outputs

* Submission-ready manuscript.

---

## Core Philosophy

Never start from an algorithm and search for a problem.

Start from a real bottleneck.

Identify why existing methods fail.

Design a mechanism to overcome that bottleneck.

Demonstrate through evidence that the mechanism is responsible for the improvement.
